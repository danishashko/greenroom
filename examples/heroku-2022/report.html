Rehearsal report

59% (58 to 81%) of the audience reacts negatively and 36% (32 to 57%) would leave or switch. Main objection: paid plans cost too much.

Heroku ends all free dynos, free Postgres and free Redis on November 28, 2022 and deletes inactive accounts

If this ships as announced, roughly a third of the hobby audience moves to Render, Railway or Fly.io before November 28. Most of the rest are already elsewhere or on paid plans, and they mostly shrug. No one is pleased. The main reasons are that paid plans feel too expensive for projects that earn nothing, and that deleting inactive accounts raises the fear of losing data. Rivals with generous free tiers and smooth migrations make leaving easy, and this year's security incidents and outages had already worn down trust in Heroku.

What to change

  1. Announce a low-cost hobby tier at the same time as the free tier ends, with dyno, Postgres and Redis bundled into one small monthly price, rather than only removing free plans.

    The top objection, raised by 71% of the audience, is that paid plans cost too much for side projects. Rivals use usage-based or generous free pricing to win these buyers.

    Hobbyists like Marcus, who like Heroku but won't pay current prices, get a reason to stay, so fewer of them switch.

  2. Replace 'deletes inactive accounts' with 'archives inactive apps.' Before anything is removed, send an emailed one-click Postgres export, offer a longer grace period, and allow a paid restore.

    59% of the audience fear losing inactive apps and data. Deletion wording also feeds the existing distrust.

    The data-loss objection shrinks, and the announcement reads as a policy change rather than a threat.

  3. Launch a student and education program with free credits and no credit card required, verified through a school email or a bootcamp partner.

    Students chose Heroku because no card was needed, and that free tier made Heroku a hub for teaching Rails and Node.js.

    Students like Priya keep learning on Heroku and don't have to find a new home before November 28.

  4. Explain plainly why the change is happening (abuse, security, reliability investment) and pair it with concrete commitments and an in-dashboard upgrade path, so users aren't left to migrate on their own.

    59% of the audience distrust Heroku and vendor free tiers, and rivals already have a reputation for smooth first-push migrations.

    Fewer people read the change as neglect, and more of those already considering a switch stay and upgrade.

Most at risk: Elena R., Full-stack freelancer deploying client prototypes. Elena is the largest segment (19%), already runs new deploys smoothly on Render's free plan and takes this announcement as the push to move all her client prototypes away.
